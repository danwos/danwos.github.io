## Why Horus

A Sphinx-Needs project quickly grows to a few hundred requirements,
specifications and tests. As a table or graph, that soon gets hard to read:
you see single rows and arrows, but no feel for how big an area is, where
things cluster and where the gaps are.

With Horus I wanted to try whether a spatial view helps. The idea: data as a
city you move through like a place you know – inspired by the neon world of
the TRON movies.

## Every need a floor

Every tower is a group of needs, every floor a single need with its ID and
title. The color shows the status. You decide how the city is built: cities,
districts and towers can be grouped by type, tag, folder or status. In the
Sphinx-Needs demo, for example, the folders become three cities and the
types their districts.

<Screen src={city} alt="The Sphinx-Needs demo as a city: one tower per need type, the floors glow in the color of their status." />

You fly through the city with keyboard and mouse, or jump straight to a
city, a district or a need from the navigator. Links between needs run as
glowing arcs from tower to tower.

## One click, and you're at the need

A click on a floor opens the need with its content, metadata and all its
links. Click a link and the camera flies there. A double click shows the
full content with tables and lists.

<Screen src={select} alt="A software requirement is selected. Its content and links are on the right; in the city, the links run as arcs to other towers." />

## Errors show from far away

When Sphinx-Needs checks your needs against a schema, Horus shows the
violations right in the city. Affected floors and districts glow red, the
navigator counts them, and the need tells you which rule it breaks.

<Screen src={problems} alt="Color scheme “Problems”: single floors in the tower of functional safety requirements glow red. On the right, the message that a safety-critical requirement has no link." />

## At street level and from above

From the street you see how the towers stand to each other. From high above,
the city turns into a flat map where every area is as large as its group.

<Screen src={ride} alt="Street-level view across a city of glowing towers." />

## Status

Horus is a prototype from spring 2026 and runs in the browser. It reads any
`needs.json` the Sphinx-Needs needs builder creates – besides the demo also
the roughly 1,400 needs of Eclipse S-CORE. I did not develop it further: I
lacked the time to try it out with users and customers and shape it to
their needs. The code is not public.
