## Why deciwa

<Scene monster="deciwa-desk" alt="deciwa at a desk, checking a stack of papers" />

AI writes requirements, docs, websites and code in minutes. You still have to
check it yourself. Your feedback ends up in a chat, in pull request comments
or in an email. After three rounds nobody knows which statement you actually
confirmed and which one the AI has rewritten since.

I know this from my own work: in my knowledge base the AI writes a lot, and I
approve. I wanted a tool where I point at a spot, say briefly what I think of
it – and that remembers what I confirmed.

<video src="/videos/deciwa-product-en.mp4" poster="/videos/deciwa-product-en.jpg" autoplay muted loop playsinline controls preload="metadata"></video>

You can try deciwa right away: the [deciwa docs](https://deciwa.danwos.dev) can
be reviewed with deciwa itself. Your review stays in your browser.

## Feedback right on the result

You review what comes out at the end: the built docs, later the finished
website too. Turn on review and deciwa outlines the element under the cursor –
a paragraph, a table row, a picture, a requirement. A click opens its card. If
you only want a few words, select them first.

Next to every element with feedback, a colored bar shows where it stands:
approved, rejected, frozen, changed after approval, or with an open question.

## Three ways to work with deciwa

### Decide

A verdict says what should happen to a spot. **Approve** confirms the
statement, **Reject** says it is wrong, **Approve & freeze** confirms it and
protects it from any change, **Delete** is the job to remove it. What exactly
should change you simply write in the comment – it goes along with the
verdict. **Comment only** just keeps a thought. One click is enough, and every
button has a key.

### Rate the quality

The zero to five stars are separate from that. With them you quickly say how
good you think a spot is – without deciding anything. A statement can be
correct and still get only two stars because it is clumsily written. Later you
see where the weak spots are.

### Talk to the AI

If the AI – Claude, for example – is unsure about a spot, it asks you a
question right there instead of guessing. You answer with **Yes**, **No** or in
a few words, and the AI carries on.

## Everything at a glance

<Screen src={panel} float alt="The panel for a page: share with a verdict and with stars, the review rule, open, rejected, changed and approved elements." />

The panel shows all feedback – for the page or for the whole project. At the
top you see how many of the elements you have to approve already have a
verdict and how many got stars. You decide what must be approved: all
Sphinx-Needs requirements, for example, all table rows, or everything. These
elements carry a gray dashed line until you have decided.

A click on an entry jumps to that spot, on another page too.

## Approved stays approved – until the text changes

An approval is tied to exactly the element you confirmed, not to a line
number. If someone changes the text later, deciwa notices. The element is
marked "stale", you see the changed words, and you decide again. That way it
is always clear which statements are really confirmed right now.

<Screen src={stale} alt="A requirement was changed after its approval: the card shows “stale” and, in its history, the old and the new word." />

## Freeze: hands off

<Scene monster="deciwa-furious" alt="deciwa, furious with raised fists" left />

Some parts must stay exactly as they are: legal text, an agreed requirement, a
constant in code. Mark them with **Approve & freeze**. The AI leaves them
alone – and if the text changes anyway, deciwa raises the alarm and shows you
what was there before.

## Go through it without missing anything

The **guided review** walks you through everything that has no verdict yet:
the card opens on the first open element, and after each decision it moves on
to the next – first on the page, then on the following pages.

<Screen src={guided} alt="Guided review: the panel shows the open elements, the card is already on the next requirement." />

## The AI works through your feedback

<Scene monster="deciwa-high-five" alt="deciwa and danwos giving each other a high five" />

Every piece of feedback is a clear instruction: the comment says what should
change, Reject means fix it, Delete means remove it, Freeze means hands off.
The AI fetches the open list from the command line, works through it, checks
each item off and asks its questions right at the element.

deciwa itself never changes text. It collects your feedback, evaluates it and
shows what is still missing. The work is done by the AI or by you.

## Where you use deciwa

- **Sphinx** – now: on the built docs, with Sphinx-Needs, Markdown and
  reStructuredText
- **VS Code** – in the editor and in the preview of your docs (planned)
- **Chrome** – on any rendered page, docs or website (planned)
- **Claude** – you go through a file together with the AI, and Claude asks its
  questions right in deciwa
- **Desktop app** – for everyone who doesn't use a development environment
  (later)

Everywhere you get the same buttons, the same states and the same data.
Review works on a phone too: tap instead of point.

## Where your data lives

Your feedback never ends up in your files. deciwa stores everything in a
`.deciwa` folder – in your repository, or in a folder above it if you review
several repositories together. The format is openly documented and versioned
with Git, like the rest of your project.

Without a server, the review runs in the browser. You export what you rated
there as one file and bring it into the repository later.

For teams there will be a hosted service. Data lives there centrally, and you
get a task list per person, an evaluation of your stars that shows the weak
spots, comparisons between versions and a report for audits: who approved
which wording when.

## Status

deciwa runs as a prototype for Sphinx docs – the pictures here come straight
