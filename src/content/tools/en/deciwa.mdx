## Why deciwa

<Scene monster="deciwa-desk" alt="deciwa at a desk, checking a stack of papers" />

AI writes requirements, docs, websites and code in minutes. You still have to
check it yourself. Your feedback ends up in a chat, in pull request comments
or in an email. After three rounds nobody knows which statement you actually
confirmed and which one the AI has rewritten since.

I know this from my own work: in my knowledge base the AI writes a lot, and I
approve. I wanted a tool where I point at a spot, say briefly what I think of
it – and that remembers what I confirmed.

## Feedback right on the result

You review what comes out at the end: the built docs, the finished website.
Move the mouse over it and deciwa outlines the element under the cursor – a
paragraph, a card, a requirement. Hold Alt to jump one level up. If you only
want a single word, select it.

<Screen src={website} alt="deciwa on a website: on a feature card the AI asks whether freeze should be mentioned there too. The answer is in the comment field." />

## Three ways to work with deciwa

### Give feedback

Feedback says what should happen to a spot. **Approve** confirms the
statement, **Reject** says it is wrong. **Rephrase**, **More details** and
**Delete** are jobs for the AI: reword, expand, remove. **Freeze** protects a
spot from any change, and **Note** just keeps a thought. One click is enough;
a comment is optional.

### Rate the quality

The one to five stars are separate from that. With them you quickly say how
good you think a spot is – without deciding anything or giving a job. A
statement can be correct and still get only two stars because it is clumsily
written. Later you see where the weak spots are and put your time exactly
there.

### Talk to the AI

If the AI – Claude, for example – is unsure about a spot, it asks you a
question right there instead of guessing. The question shows up in deciwa on
the element it is about. You answer with one click, where Approve means "yes,
that's fine", or you write an answer. The AI reads it and carries on.

You find all three on the card: the AI's question at the top, the stars below
it, then the feedback buttons and the comment field.

## Everything at a glance

<Screen src={list} float alt="The side panel with all feedback on the file: approved, changes requested, question open, note and frozen." />

Next to the page, deciwa lists all feedback on the file: what is approved, what
should change, where a question from the AI is open and what is frozen, each
with your stars. You find the same colors on the edge of the page, right next
to the parts they belong to.

At the top you see how much of the file is already covered by an approval. A
click on an entry jumps to that spot – so you go through a file from top to
bottom without missing anything.

## Approved stays approved – until the text changes

An approval is tied to exactly the element you confirmed, not to a line
number. If someone changes the text later, deciwa notices. The element is
marked "stale", you see what changed, and you decide again. That way it is
always clear which statements are really confirmed right now.

## Freeze: hands off

<Scene monster="deciwa-furious" alt="deciwa, furious with raised fists" left />

Some parts must stay exactly as they are: legal text, an agreed requirement, a
constant in code. Mark them with **Freeze**. The AI leaves them alone – and if
the text changes anyway, deciwa raises the alarm and shows you what was there
before.

<Screen src={code} alt="A frozen constant in Python code was changed anyway. deciwa reports “frozen, but changed!” and shows the old value." />

## The AI works through your feedback

<Scene monster="deciwa-high-five" alt="deciwa and danwos giving each other a high five" />

Every piece of feedback is a clear instruction: Rephrase means reword it, More
details means expand it, Delete means remove it, Freeze means hands off. The
AI fetches the open list, works through it and then asks you to look at the
changed parts again.

deciwa itself never changes text. It collects your feedback, evaluates it and
shows what is still missing. The work is done by the AI or by you.

## Where you use deciwa

- **VS Code** – in the editor and in the preview of your docs
- **Chrome** – on any rendered page, docs or website
- **Claude** – you go through a file together with the AI in the chat, and
  Claude asks its questions right in deciwa
- **Desktop app** – for everyone who doesn't use a development environment

deciwa understands Markdown, reStructuredText with Sphinx-Needs, AsciiDoc,
HTML, plain text and code in Python, C, JavaScript and TypeScript. Everywhere
you get the same buttons and the same states.

## Where your data lives

Your feedback never ends up in your files. deciwa stores everything in a
`.deciwa` folder – in your repository, or in a folder above it if you review
several repositories together. The format is openly documented and versioned
with Git, like the rest of your project.

For teams there is a hosted service. Data lives there centrally, and you get a
task list per person, an overview of how much is already confirmed, an
evaluation of your stars that shows the weak spots, comparisons between
versions and a report for audits: who approved which wording when.
